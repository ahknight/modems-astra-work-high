THE BREAKTHROUGH  /  1991

Compare two good connections. At 9,600 bit/s, every symbol carried four user bits. At 14,400 it carried six. The carrier still sat at 1,800 Hz, with each end cancelling its echo. The CCITT’s 1991 V.32bis recommendation used 128 possible amplitude-and-phase points at top speed. Six information bits plus a bit generated by the trellis encoder tell the transmitter which point to send. The extra bit gives the receiver clues across symbols. Lee-Fang Wei’s rotationally invariant trellis work underlies this family of modems. 12

The startup has a revealing change of texture. The receiver hears a regular pattern, a short reversal, then what sounds like a rough-edged hiss. Both ends can generate that last sequence from a rule. They can therefore tell how much of its roughness the telephone line added. Inside a real modem, the receiver uses it to tune its equalizer and the sender can use it to learn its own echo. 1

A V.32bis machine had to implement 4,800, 7,200, 9,600, 12,000 and 14,400 bit/s. The two ends negotiated a common rate and could renegotiate while connected without repeating all of training. Compatibility with V.32 at 4,800 and 9,600 preserved the older caller’s place on the bulletin board. 1

FIELD NOTE

Why text could download faster than a ZIP

Optional layers changed file throughput. V.42’s LAPM checked frames and retried damaged ones. V.42bis, used over an error-controlled link, replaced recurring strings with dictionary codes and could switch to transparent transmission for incompressible data. The wire still carried the negotiated number of bits per second.

Listening transcript

Select a stage to move the playhead. Every description stays here as the signal plays.

  1. The answering modem sends 2,100 Hz with half-turn phase reversals, a request to disable network echo cancellation.

  2. The caller adds a steady 1,800 Hz. Its tone will overlap the answering modem’s signals.

  3. The answerer sends 600 and 3,000 Hz together. The ends take turns reversing phase, timing the replies to estimate delay.

  4. A regular S pattern and its brief reversal set the timing. The signal then opens into a reproducible stream of scrambled ones for echo and equalizer training.

  5. The answering modem repeats a 16-bit rate word offering all five V.32bis speeds, including 14,400.

  6. The caller supplies its own known sequence. Each direction uses a different scrambler so the two signals can be distinguished.

  7. While the caller repeats its rate offer, the answerer trains again and selects 14,400. Both transmit an end word. The short data-mode preamble that follows is outside this excerpt.

How this signal was made

Synthesis begins at V.25 answer tone, after dialing and the initial off-hook silence. The caller answers after 1.02 s. V.25 permits early termination after detecting its response for 100 ms, so the 1.2 s tone is a permitted example. One-way delay is 10 ms. Training lengths and finite root-raised-cosine transmit filters are example choices. No channel or receiver is simulated. Ends at the E words, before trellis-coded B1 and user data.
